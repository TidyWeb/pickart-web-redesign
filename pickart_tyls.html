Thank Your Lucky Stars · The Merseybeat Special

The night Britain
lost its head.

15 December 1963. A converted cinema in Birmingham. Four songs, a snowstorm, extra police at the doors — and She Loves You to close the show. Beatlemania wasn't coming. It had arrived.

Alpha Studios, Aston, Birmingham · Seven frames survive

One year. Two bookings.

From bottom of the bill to the top —
in eleven months.

Thank Your Lucky Stars was ITV's answer to Juke Box Jury, and it made one very good decision: it boarded the Merseybeat train early. What happened to The Beatles between their two 1963 bookings is the whole story of Beatlemania, told in a running order.

13 January 1963
Bottom of the bill · one song

Their first appearance on national television. Time for a single number: Please Please Me. Nobody outside Liverpool knew their names yet. That lasted about a week.

15 December 1963
Top of the bill · four songs

The Merseybeat Special. I Want To Hold Your Hand. All My Loving. Twist And Shout. And She Loves You to play the show out — while extra police fought the crowds outside in the snow.

The man in the room

Photographed by the only man allowed to be there.

Willoughby 'Gus' Gullachsen learned his trade as an RAF photographer in South Africa, India and Iraq, then came home to Birmingham and became the man the theatres, the press and ABC Weekend Television called first. As Thank Your Lucky Stars' official photographer, he stood where no fan, no reporter and no rival camera could — feet from the stage, mid-performance.

Every photograph in this collection comes from his private archive, taken during the performance of She Loves You on the night of the Merseybeat Special. They have never been published. Until now, they had never been printed.
